# References: Capstone Projects and Career Development

1. [Capstone course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capstone_course) - Wikipedia - Describes the culminating, self-directed project format common to undergraduate and graduate programs, matching this chapter's framing of the capstone as a synthesis of the entire curriculum.

2. [Scope (project management)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scope_(project_management)) - Wikipedia - Explains defining project boundaries and deliverables, including scope creep, directly supporting the chapter's emphasis on narrowing an ambitious idea into an achievable, bounded capstone scope.

3. [Project risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_risk_management) - Wikipedia - Covers identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks before they derail a project, matching the chapter's risk register example addressing sparse synthetic data and slow live-demo algorithms.

4. Cracking the Coding Interview: 189 Programming Questions and Solutions (6th Edition) - Gayle Laakmann McDowell - CareerCup - McDowell is credited with establishing the modern structured technical-interview-preparation methodology -- rehearsed problem-solving and complexity analysis -- that the chapter's interview preparation section draws on.

5. What Color Is Your Parachute? (Annual Edition) - Richard N. Bolles - Ten Speed Press - Bolles is credited with pioneering the informational-interview-based, relationship-driven job search method over mass resume submission, the same networking-first philosophy the chapter recommends for launching a graph modeling career.

6. [Project Management (MIT 1.040)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-040-project-management-spring-2004/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - A free university course on planning, organizing, and controlling projects that supports the chapter's project scoping and stakeholder-requirements guidance with formal project-management theory.

7. [How to Contribute to Open Source](https://opensource.guide/how-to-contribute/) - Open Source Guides (GitHub) - A practical guide to making a first open-source contribution, directly supporting the chapter's advice that a small, well-documented contribution builds a credible, publicly verifiable portfolio record.

8. [Neo4j GraphAcademy Certifications](https://graphacademy.neo4j.com/certifications/) - Neo4j - Describes free, vendor-specific graph database certifications, giving concrete substance to the chapter's discussion of industry certification as a portfolio supplement for graph modeling careers.

9. [Graph Databases for Fraud Detection & Analytics](https://neo4j.com/use-cases/fraud-detection/) - Neo4j - A real-world case-study resource on graph-based fraud detection that students can cite in the chapter's case study analysis exercise to situate a fraud-detection capstone within established industry practice.

10. [Interview Preparation Roadmap](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/blogs/interview-preparation-roadmap/) - GeeksforGeeks - A structured roadmap covering technical and soft-skill interview preparation that complements the chapter's guidance on rehearsing a capstone project's scoping decisions and results as an interview narrative.
